# Art direction — reference-led Folk
Updated 2026-10-19. The user's October 19 concept board supersedes the earlier directional-v47 character authority. The active gold standard is now `assets/folk-v54`, rendered by `scripts/folk.gd` at gameplay scale. Original assets remain available for recovery and historical comparison.

Use broad, sturdy adult Dwarfs: substantial brows and noses, compact powerful bodies, expressive faces, dense beards/braids, weighty boots, leather and restrained brass. Warm illustrated contours and readable hand-painted volume; avoid anime/chibi proportions, glossy plastic, photorealistic texture noise and tiny illegible ornament. Seven male and seven female identity variants share clothing, materials, scale and directional registration.

Complete drawn body keys communicate anticipation, contact and recovery. Keep the chosen head, pigment and clothing consistent across movement, work, carry, NPC activity, creator and dialogue. No rotating detached limb substitutes. Hands visibly support actual cargo and grip tools. Match the tool's contact to the work surface; do not hide bad reach behind automatic tunnelling.

Use one recognizable raw rock/log drawing on the ground, shoulders, station, stockpile and cart. Draw quantity changes from actual load records. Existing resources, stations and environment art must be judged beside the new Dwarf, not in isolation. Painted architectural door centres and collision boundaries must agree.

Keldrath should read as a mountain hold: load-bearing stone, vaulted depth, warm inhabited windows, clear thresholds, substantial terraces and working districts. The .54 facade/vault pass is a foundation, not completion of the requested larger connected world or every interior.

Portraits and cutscenes may be more cinematic, but preserve identity. Creator busts now use the same head/body art at full source resolution. Review native scenes, complete walk/work keys, loaded/empty states, all facings, and menus before acceptance. User acceptance of feel and visual quality remains the final standard.
