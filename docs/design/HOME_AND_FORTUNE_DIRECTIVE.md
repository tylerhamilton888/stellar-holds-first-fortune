# THE STELLAR HOLDS: THE FIRST FORTUNE
## A Home and a Fortune
### Remaining Development Directive — Post 0.113.0

**Project:** The Stellar Holds: The First Fortune  
**Repository:** `tylerhamilton888/stellar-holds-first-fortune`  
**Baseline:** Current master, following verified Windows 0.113.0

## 1. Central objective

Our goal is to make The Stellar Holds feel like a Dwarven homesteading sandbox in which the player develops a deeply personal attachment to a persistent property.

The experiential comparison is the player's farm in Stardew Valley or homestead in Valheim.

However, our player is a Dwarf. Their ambitions revolve around mining, masonry, smithing, woodworking, engineering, household life, and eventually the construction of a magnificent personal stronghold.

The player should be able to pursue these ambitions indefinitely, independently of the main storyline.

**The player should not feel compelled to build a homestead to progress the campaign. The campaign should unfold naturally because the player is building a life, developing their skills, and establishing themselves within Stonebridge.**

This directive concerns the remaining story, progression, and gameplay improvements. Do not rebuild the room blueprinting, underground planning, connected floors, worker navigation, or freight functionality already delivered.

---

## 2. Revised opening: A gift from home

### The premise

The player is a young Dwarf who has recently come of age.

Their parents are ordinary, hardworking Dwarfs who have spent years saving money to help their child establish a household of their own.

They cannot afford an elaborate manor, thriving forge, or fashionable residence in a great hold.

Instead, they purchase a modest property in the growing frontier settlement of Stonebridge.

**The Old Stonewright's Rise.**

The property was advertised enthusiastically:

*"Sound mountain foundations! Established quarry and masonry yard! Traditional stone dwelling! Natural springwater! Excellent opportunities for expansion!"*

Technically, the description is accurate.

However, the cottage is weathered, the yard is neglected, the quarry has been abandoned, and significant work is needed before the holding becomes truly prosperous.

This should be humorous and endearing rather than a story about fraud or deliberate parental cruelty.

### The Dwarven cultural philosophy

In Dwarven society, providing a young adult with the beginnings of a household is a meaningful family tradition.

The greatest gift is not necessarily a finished home. It is sound ground, an honest opportunity, and room to build something worthy of future generations.

The player's parents have not intentionally purchased worthless land. They recognize the potential of a place that needs work.

Their contribution is significant but modest.

**The parents supply the beginning. The player creates the fortune.**

### Ivar's revised role

Ivar is no longer the person who generously grants the player their original property.

Instead, he is Stonebridge's respected foreman and local guide, expecting the arrival of a young settler whose family purchased the old holding.

He introduces the player to the settlement, helps them understand the property and offers opportunities for honest work.

He may find the property's optimistic advertising amusing, but he genuinely considers it promising.

Ivar should remain warm, practical, grounded, and distinctly Dwarven.

### The opening sequence

1. The player arrives in Stonebridge carrying a letter from their parents and the papers for their holding.
2. They meet Ivar, who introduces the settlement and explains the way to the eastern rise.
3. The player visits the Old Stonewright's Rise almost immediately.
4. They discover the humble cottage, neglected yard, and substantial mountainside.
5. They can inspect the property, use its modest facilities, and begin improving it.
6. Ivar mentions paid mining work, local trades, and the Hold's Ledger as opportunities to earn money, acquire materials, and establish themselves.
7. The player is free to begin their own projects rather than being forced immediately into a mining tutorial.

**Target experience:** The player should reach their property within approximately the first five to ten minutes of a normal new game.

Keep the introduction brief, characterful, skippable, and safe to save/reload.

### Letters from the parents

Introduce an initial letter expressing their hopes for the player and describing the property somewhat more optimistically than its condition warrants.

Future occasional letters may acknowledge major accomplishments.

Keep them warm, amusing, and personal. They should not become recurring obligations or deadlines.

The parents are not secretly arranging the player's destiny. Their purchase is unrelated to the later ancestral-claim revelation.

## 3. Immediate ownership and household progression

The player's original property should be **theirs from the beginning**.

It was purchased by their family.

Do not continue portraying the holding as land that Ivar may eventually choose to give them.

The existing provisional-use system in 0.113.0 should be reconciled with this new story.

### Distinguish property ownership from civic recognition

**Personal ownership:** The player already possesses the original plot and can live, build, excavate, furnish, and develop it from the beginning.

**Recognized Stonebridge household:** Contributions to the settlement establish the player as a respected householder and unlock appropriate civic privileges.

The existing two-contribution milestone can become formal recognition or household registration rather than the purchase or acquisition of land already owned.

Potential rewards include new civic opportunities, contracts, and appropriate development rights. Avoid making basic home improvements require civic permission.

Preserve the existing household site, construction, storage, workers, and progression records.

Do not create a second estate.

Existing saves with provisional or permanent tenure must retain all possessions and progress. Migration should be additive and must not revoke established rights.

### A modest initial dwelling

The player should have somewhere usable to sleep and store their belongings on their first night.

The cottage can be plain, weathered, and sparsely furnished, with obvious potential for improvement.

It should not begin as a fully functional industrial estate.

Avoid giving away advanced workstations, substantial resources, or expensive buildings.

The first meaningful construction improvements should still be the player's own accomplishments.

Reconcile the existing home-registration milestone with the starter dwelling so players receive credit for genuine improvements without being forced to demolish and reconstruct what they already own.

## 4. Reorient the first hour around personal ambition

The current first paid shift should remain an important, attractive opportunity.

However, it should no longer be the mandatory gateway to beginning the homestead experience.

The player has a house that needs work. Ivar offers wages and practical instruction that can help.

Mining becomes compelling because the player wants stone, timber, money, tools, or equipment for their own projects.

### Early opportunities

A player might choose to:

- Repair or furnish their cottage.
- Restore a small section of the masonry yard.
- Gather resources for a first workshop.
- Begin planning additional rooms.
- Investigate the quarry face.
- Pursue paid work or local trade.

These are alternative ambitions, not simultaneous mandatory tutorial objectives.

The first shift may remain the recommended introduction to physical mining and material processing.

But the player should be able to explore their property and begin useful work without completing that shift.

### The Ledger

Introduce the Ledger as a source of worthwhile community opportunities, not as the player's immediate compulsory objective list.

Ivar can point it out naturally.

Its projects should encourage players to develop their own capabilities while improving Stonebridge.

Keep the early journal focused on the player's actual chosen activity.

Do not overwhelm the opening with later campaign acts, technological requirements, or distant story milestones.

## 5. Meaningful tool and material progression

Expand the relationship between tools, materials, research, and production.

We already possess basic rock hardness, the steel-pick/basalt requirement, tool and equipment crafting, research, and workshop production.

**Extend these existing systems rather than replacing or duplicating them.**

### Mining capabilities

Better picks and relevant techniques should enable work on increasingly difficult rock and support more ambitious excavation.

Material hardness should have meaningful consequences for the player's personal building ambitions.

Basic construction and ordinary mining must remain useful.

### Smithing capabilities

Certain advanced metals and alloys should require suitable equipment and knowledge.

A sophisticated metal may require a combination of appropriate forge temperatures, hammer capability, anvil construction, and metallurgical knowledge.

An ordinary hammer should not necessarily be capable of working every material.

Upgrading the workshop should have an actual mechanical purpose.

### Masonry capabilities

Advanced stoneworking should enable superior finishes, harder stone shaping, specialized supports, impressive arches, and increasingly ambitious construction.

The player should eventually feel capable of accomplishing things that were physically beyond their abilities when they first arrived.

### Requirements in blueprints

The existing Blueprint Book should explain what an ambitious construction project requires.

Players may plan rooms and structures beyond their present capabilities.

Unavailable techniques or materials should prevent only the construction steps that genuinely require them.

Do not prevent the player from saving an ambitious future design.

## 6. Interconnected craftsmanship

Develop more meaningful relationships between existing professions.

The objective is to create a believable Dwarven economy of crafts.

### Woodworking

Woodworking should produce useful intermediate components such as:

- Tool shafts and hafts.
- Shaped boards.
- Structural beams.
- Reinforced timber frames.
- Joinery and furniture components.
- Bellows frames and workshop fittings.

### Smithing

Smithing should produce toolheads, reinforcement collars, fasteners, metal fittings, and machinery components.

### Masonry

Masonry should produce prepared building materials, structural stonework, pillars, arches, and decorative components.

### Engineering

Engineering combines suitable products from multiple crafts into advanced equipment and infrastructure.

### Example: A superior pickaxe

Producing an advanced pick might require a properly forged head, a shaped hardwood haft, and suitable fittings.

The player may gather and process the materials, manufacture the parts, and assemble the finished tool.

That tool then unlocks previously inaccessible rock, providing new resources and construction opportunities.

### Example: An advanced forge

A superior forge might require shaped masonry, a suitable metal firebox, woodworking components for its bellows, and specialist fittings.

The result is a persistent functional improvement to the player's personal workshop.

### Complexity limits

Do not add excessive intermediate components to everyday recipes.

Basic tools and furnishings should remain straightforward.

Reserve meaningful multistage production for advanced equipment, machinery, and exceptional craftsmanship.

Existing automation and hired labor should eventually reduce repetitive work without making personal crafting obsolete.

---

## 7. Multiple ways to acquire what the player needs

Not every important item or technique should come from a single mandatory research menu.

Support meaningful alternative acquisition routes.

### Self-production

The player learns and performs the necessary crafts.

This generally offers advantages in personal experience, customization, and reduced cash expense.

### Purchasing

The player buys finished goods, tools, or components from appropriate merchants.

Purchasing should be convenient and legitimate, although frequently more expensive than self-production.

A skilled carpenter or farmer should be able to earn money through their chosen occupation and purchase equipment from a smith.

### Specialist commissions

The player may pay an NPC craftsperson or provide materials for specialist manufacturing.

This allows access to expertise without requiring the player to personally master every profession.

### Apprenticeships and personal quests

An NPC may teach a technique through a relevant professional commission or story.

These should involve believable problems and enduring rewards.

### Exploration and discovery

Abandoned workshops, old schematics, geological discoveries, and forgotten machinery may reveal additional capabilities.

### Essential safeguard

Every capability essential to ordinary progression must have at least one reliable, permanently available acquisition route.

Optional friendships should provide attractive opportunities without becoming compulsory barriers.

Prevent circular recipes and technology deadlocks.

A tool must not require itself, directly or indirectly, to be constructed.

## 8. NPCs as specialists and mentors

Expand the practical significance of established NPCs rather than introducing a large new cast.

Characters such as Borin, Ivar, and suitable other craftsmen should have professional expertise reflected in actual gameplay.

### Example: Borin's reinforced hammer commission

Borin is attempting to develop a heavy masonry hammer, but conventional hafts are failing under the impact.

The player helps investigate the problem.

They may produce suitable woodworking components, purchase them, or commission another specialist.

The player works with Borin to complete the hammer.

The lasting reward is access to a useful reinforced-tool technique, as well as appropriate relationship development.

Such quests should accomplish several things simultaneously:

- Develop an established character.
- Demonstrate a practical crafting challenge.
- Introduce useful mechanics naturally.
- Unlock a persistent capability.
- Provide meaningful reasons to return to that profession later.

Avoid creating dozens of superficial material-fetching errands.

Use a small number of distinctive, memorable professional quests as the initial implementation.

## 9. Homestead, Ledger, and campaign integration

The player's personal holding should remain the center of their long-term ambitions.

Stonebridge should provide opportunities that make developing the holding more rewarding.

### Mutual benefits

A private woodworking workshop makes supplying community construction projects more convenient.

A restored public forge may provide specialist knowledge or useful services.

A restored crossing may improve access to trade and material transportation.

A prosperous private enterprise may strengthen the player's reputation in Stonebridge.

Where possible, contributions should produce tangible, persistent improvements rather than only recognition points.

### Existing campaign structure

Preserve the authored Skarrin contest, rescue, ancestry revelation, Keldrath invasion, trial, and Thane homecoming.

However, review where introductory profession requirements act as arbitrary mandatory gates.

The player should not necessarily have to master unrelated crafts before being recognized as capable.

Broader personal achievements and civic contributions can establish reputation.

Specific missions may still require appropriate physical preparations.

For example, an underground rescue may legitimately require supports, tools, and a safe route.

**Allow the story to recognize work already completed.**

Do not require players to repeat meaningful accomplishments solely because they performed them before accepting a quest.

### Skarrin

Retain his initially welcoming but gradually insincere character development.

The player's growing estate provides an additional visible reason for his jealousy.

If compatible with established history, consider revealing that Skarrin once declined the opportunity to develop the old Stonewright's Rise.

This should remain a character detail, not a major new mandatory plotline.

### The eventual Thane

The player must retain the same personal holding after receiving Thaneship.

It may eventually become a magnificent private manor, industrial estate, or underground stronghold.

Do not replace it with a standardized noble residence.

The player's history should remain visible in the place they personally built.

## 11. Quality and compatibility requirements

All development must preserve established project safeguards.

In particular:

- Preserve existing saves, property, furnishings, inventories, workers, and story progression.
- Do not overwrite or remove prior releases.
- Keep the current Blueprint Book, connected levels, navigation, and freight systems authoritative.
- Reuse existing crafting, research, equipment, and material ownership systems.
- Make ownership-state transitions safe for provisional, permanent, and legacy players.
- Validate the new opening through normal player input, not only direct state manipulation.
- Test buying, crafting, quest rewards, and alternate paths for duplication or deadlocks.
- Keep construction progress and materials persistent.

- Use focused tests, visual review, and the established release validation process.
- Keep development summaries and tool output bounded.

Do not mark a feature delivered until its implementation and release status are verified.

## 12. Definition of success

A new player should arrive in Stonebridge and almost immediately discover that they possess a humble holding purchased through their parents' sacrifice.

The property looks a little worse than expected.

But its potential is tremendous.

The player can begin making it their own without first completing a long sequence of mandatory tutorials.

They need materials, money, tools, knowledge, and eventually skilled workers.

Those needs naturally lead them toward mining, woodworking, smithing, exploration, NPC relationships, trade, and the Hold's Ledger.

As they grow more capable, their property visibly improves.

Their personal ambitions lead to community accomplishments.

Their growing reputation draws them into the larger story.

And their story accomplishments create new opportunities to further develop the place they call home.

**The player should be capable of enjoying the homestead indefinitely, whether they pursue Thaneship immediately, eventually, or hardly at all.**
